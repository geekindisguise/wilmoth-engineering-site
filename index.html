    Jacob Wilmoth, electrical engineer

    I convert old cars to electric, then write the software to see inside them.

    Two daily-driven conversions, a J1772 charge controller on the bench, apps for iPhone, iPad and Mac, and a camera along for all of it.

    This is a typical conversion, drawn live. Scroll to power it up.

    Step 1

    12 V wakes the bus

    Key on. The 12 V system wakes every controller, and each one starts talking on the CAN bus: two twisted wires with a 120 Ω resistor at each end.

    Step 2

    The BMS checks the pack

    The battery management system reads every module and puts pack voltage, temperatures and state of charge on the bus as CAN frames.

    Step 3

    Precharge

    The pack has a contactor on each side. The negative one, K2, closes first. Then a resistor across the positive contactor charges the controller’s capacitors slowly. Close straight into an empty capacitor bank and the inrush can weld the contacts.

    Step 4

    Contactors closed

    With the DC link near pack voltage, the positive contactor, K1, closes too, so both sides of the pack are connected. High voltage (orange, as it is in every EV) reaches the motor controller and the DC-DC converter that keeps the 12 V side charged.

    Step 5

    Drive

    The motor controller turns DC into three-phase AC for the motor, and reports speed and temperatures to the dash and telemetry.

    Step 6

    Plug in

    At a J1772 station, the control-pilot signal tells the charger how much current it may draw, and current flows back into the pack. I’m building a J1772 controller from the ground up and testing it on Rosie, my 1978 Beetle.

    Step 7

    Read every frame

    Every frame on that bus can be opened in CANnery, the CAN analyzer I wrote for Mac and iPad: load a DBC, decode the signals, chart them.

      CANnery

      A native CAN bus analyzer for Mac and iPad. Import ASC, BLF or CSV logs, load DBC files, and read every decoded signal as a chart or a raw trace.

      1. Opens logs and traces, loads a DBC, decodes and charts signals.
      2. Demo Bus runs locally so you can learn the layout on the device.
      3. Plus adds live adapters, transmit, J1939, UDS, and export.
      4. No account. Logs, DBC files and bus traffic stay on your device.
